# MOONPAW visual direction

The world is a side-on illustrated horror diorama. Geometry has depth for layered silhouettes, bevels, shadows, and selective lighting, while play stays on one flat x/y plane. The camera tracks horizontally and vertically with a fixed shallow angle. Nothing rotates the camera around the player.

The value range carries the image: ink-black sky, charcoal silhouettes, bone-white platforms and moon. Neon is reserved for the cat's eyes and scarf, hazards, portals, active checkpoints, and a handful of stage motifs. A hazard should remain legible without depending on color. The cat's rendered outline fits the 0.7 by 1.1 collision footprint; its feet share the physics origin.

Each world has an architectural silhouette: graveyard rooftops, skeletal timber machinery, rain-cut high rises, abandoned cloud spires, and fractured monoliths. Watching eyes are fixed far behind the play surface. Weather moves slowly. There are no jump scares or bright full-screen flashes.

Balanced is the reference appearance. Performance caps pixel ratio at 1 and reduces particles. Cinematic caps pixel ratio at 2, enables shadows, and applies a low-strength bloom to the few emissive details. Reduced-motion preference holds the decorative movement still while keeping the player and gameplay state readable.
